# Improving spoken-blurb understanding

Speak It currently runs deterministic speech repair, clause segmentation and semantic organization, with optional Apple Foundation Models refinement. The model uses greedy decoding, a two-second budget and grounding checks. This work changes the rules; it does not train model weights.

## Measured defects

The second supplied shopping blurb originally produced six rows: an introduction, a restaurant visit, two store visits and two detached purchases. The intended result is three errands, all due today, without inventing reminder times. Store spellings remain as transcribed: Bendy Melville and Mooji cannot safely be inferred to mean other businesses.

The fixes recognize an explicitly numbered list introduction, preserve its shared day, remove a redundant sequencing word after “then”, and associate an immediately adjacent purchase with a bare visit. Sentence breaks, “then”, a separate purchase date and unrelated actions prevent that association. Existing grocery product expansion retains its own path. “Later on” remains in analysis but comes out of the visit's display title.

Regression tests cover the exact capture, lowercase and unpunctuated renderings, different destinations and objects, genuine conditions, separate dates, and independent actions. These are development examples, not held-out evidence.

## Sustainable improvement loop

1. Add reviewed input → expected intentions examples to development tests. Label names, dates, negation, intent count and source wording, not just attractive titles.
2. Keep independent evaluation captures separate. The existing `Tools/CorpusRunner/heldout/` scorer reports routing, count and ambiguous captures acted on. Inspect aggregate scores only during development. A perfect regression corpus is not proof that arbitrary speech works.
3. Compare rules alone against actual device refinement on the same evaluation set, including latency and unsupported-device fallback. Only ship model prompt or model changes after demonstrating better accuracy without lost intentions or invented reminders.
4. For future supervised training, use reviewed, permissioned transcripts and independently authored variations. Keep paraphrases from one capture in the same split to avoid evaluation leakage. Competitor behavior may inform product scenarios, but another app's output is not an authoritative label or access to its model weights.

## Training decision

Apple's current adapter page says toolkit 26.0.0 is its final release and is incompatible with iOS/macOS 27 and later. Investing in that adapter route is therefore unsuitable as the app's long-term training strategy. No training job, model download, private-data upload or paid inference was started.

If measured errors persist after rule and prompt improvements, evaluate a separately licensed trainable model against the same held-out contract before choosing hardware, hosting or a training budget. Do not replace the current parser merely because a model sounds more fluent.

Source checked September 10, 2026: https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/

## Validation

- Exact supplied second blurb: six rows before, three correctly grouped errands after; all due today, no reminder times invented.
- Focused simulator tests: 22 passed, zero failures, including case/punctuation variants and non-merging controls.
- Full semantic regression corpus: 1,381/1,381 clean, no disagreements.
- Existing 389-capture held-out benchmark, evaluated once after final changes: routing 233/320 (72.8%), thought count 255/310 (82.3%), no missing outputs, seven of 69 ambiguous captures acted on. These match its previously recorded baseline. Individual held-out cases and failures were not inspected.

This demonstrates the targeted fixes and preserved existing behavior; it does not establish a broad accuracy increase on unseen speech. The seven ambiguous captures acted on remain an important product gap. Future improvement claims should include those independent metrics, not only regression-suite pass rates.
