# Speak It UGC TikTok Slideshow Playbook

## Deliverable

Five distinct UGC-style TikTok photo-carousel concepts, each with two complete
variations: **10 production-ready slideshows total**.

The intended viewer is an iPhone-owning student or young professional who has
tasks, errands, ideas, deadlines, and small personal details appear at the wrong
time, and finds conventional productivity systems too slow at the moment of
capture.

The single product promise across the campaign is:

> Say the thought once. Speak It puts actions in Today, keeps useful knowledge
> in Memory, and preserves what you originally said.

Do not turn every carousel into a feature tour. Each concept below sells one
emotion or job, then uses the product as the resolution.

## What the research says—and how this campaign uses it

1. **Hook, body, close.** TikTok's Creative Codes recommend this three-part
   structure. The first frame opens a loop, slides 2–6 resolve it, and slide 7
   asks for one action.
2. **Make it native, not polished.** TikTok recommends creator-led,
   TikTok-first work. The images should feel like one person's real camera roll:
   imperfect iPhone photos, a consistent room/day/person, and real app
   screenshots. Do not use glossy ad templates or stock-photo collages.
3. **Use text as the story, not decoration.** TikTok reports that text overlays
   can increase view time and recall. Every slide carries one short thought that
   makes the next swipe feel necessary.
4. **Reveal the product early enough to be remembered.** The human problem
   appears first; Speak It appears on slide 3 or 4. TikTok's own analysis found
   that showing the product supports brand affinity and recall.
5. **Photo carousels are a real app-acquisition format.** TikTok's Soli case
   study reports a 38% lower CPI and 1.78x higher CTR after the app adapted
   successful organic carousels for paid use. The lesson is not that those
   exact results will repeat; it is that organic carousel winners can be a
   low-cost creative testing pool before paid amplification.
6. **Creative variety matters.** TikTok recommends refreshing performance
   creative regularly and cites benefits from maintaining 5–7 assets. These ten
   posts deliberately test five different motivations instead of repeating one
   aesthetic ten times.
7. **Use relevant discovery signals.** TikTok says relevant hashtags help the
   right audience find a post, while #FYP and extra hashtags do not guarantee
   distribution. Put the searchable problem in the visible copy and caption,
   then use 3–5 specific tags.
8. **Sound still matters in Photo Mode.** TikTok Photo Mode supports music and
   lets viewers swipe at their own pace. Use a low-volume track from the
   Commercial Music Library, chosen on posting day for the target region.

## Campaign-wide production rules

### Format

- Build every image at **1080 × 1920 (9:16)**.
- Use **7 slides** per post. It is enough room for a story without padding it.
- Keep important copy away from TikTok's top, right, and bottom interface; use
  the in-app preview before publishing.
- Slide 1: 6–12 words, one emotional idea, no logo, no App Store badge.
- Slides 2–6: ideally 5–16 words each. One sentence or fragment per slide.
- Slide 7: product name, clear outcome, and exactly one CTA.
- Use sentence case or natural lowercase. Do not use corporate title case.
- Use a bold, readable sans serif with high contrast and a light shadow or
  translucent plate only when the photo needs it.

### UGC visual language

- Use one real creator per carousel. A founder can be the creator, but the post
  must sound like a founder demonstration—not a fake customer testimonial.
- Aim for roughly **70% real-life iPhone photos and 30% app screenshots**.
- Preserve ordinary details: a charging cable, half-finished coffee, wrinkled
  bedding, backpack, grocery aisle, desk clutter, transit seat. Those details
  make the story believable.
- Do not make every slide beige and aesthetic. A screenshot after two personal
  photos creates a useful pattern break.
- Crop the existing Speak It screenshots rather than placing a tiny full phone
  on a large background. The feature being discussed must be readable.
- Do not fake notifications, reviews, install counts, App Store rankings, or
  customer results.

### Existing product assets to use

- Today: `Website/assets/img/01-Today-v2.png`
- Memory: `Website/assets/img/02-Memory-v2.png`
- Capture: `Design/Screenshots/03-Capture.png`
- App icon: `SpeakIt/Assets.xcassets/AppIcon.appiconset/SpeakIt-AppIcon-1024.png`

Use the two `Website/assets` screenshots for published work: they contain the
clean marketing sample data used on the current site. Recapture any transcript
or original-wording detail needed for a specific story so the screenshot and
the creator's spoken example match exactly.

### Audio

- Choose a currently rising **Commercial Music Library** track on posting day.
- Prefer lyric-light or instrumental audio that matches the emotional angle:
  reflective for Concepts 1 and 2, lightly frantic for Concept 4, minimal and
  calm for Concepts 3 and 5.
- Keep the music supportive; the slides must work muted.
- If posting from a brand or business account, do not use a popular copyrighted
  sound merely because it is trending. Use the CML or music you have licensed.

### CTA gate

- **Before the App Store listing is live:** use “follow for the iPhone launch”
  or “join the launch list in bio.”
- **After the listing is live:** use “try 10 captures free—Speak It for iPhone.”
- Do not publish “download now,” a price, a discount, or an App Store badge until
  the live listing and localized offer have been verified.

## Concept 1 — The thought that arrives at the worst time

**Job:** Make the cost of capture friction instantly familiar.

**Creator/setting:** Young professional or student, late at night. Use the same
bedroom, bedside table, and phone across both versions. The story should feel
like a personal camera-roll confession, not a sleep-product ad.

| Slide | Visual | Variation A — emotional | Variation B — practical |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dim ceiling/bed selfie, phone glow | **my best idea this week showed up at 1:43 a.m.** | **my brain only remembers important things when my hands are full** |
| 2 | Blurry bedside table with water and charger | by morning, I could only remember that it was “really good” | if saving it takes more than one step, the thought is gone |
| 3 | Thumb opening Speak It capture screen | so now I tap once and say the whole messy thought | this is why I replaced the “which app?” decision with one button |
| 4 | Tight crop of the listening orb or live transcript | no formatting. no choosing a list. no trying to sound organized. | I say the task, idea, or reminder exactly how it arrived |
| 5 | Today screenshot, cropped around actionable rows | things I need to do show up in **Today** | action words and times land in **Today** |
| 6 | Memory screenshot, cropped around Ideas/People/Reference | ideas and details I want later stay in **Memory** | useful notes, people, and ideas stay searchable in **Memory** |
| 7 | Hand holding phone on Speak It screen; product name added | **Speak It** — for thoughts that refuse to wait until morning. *Follow for the iPhone launch.* | one place to say it before it disappears. **Speak It for iPhone.** *Follow for launch.* |

### Captions

**A**

> I needed a brain dump app that worked before I was awake enough to organize
> anything. Speak It lets me say the thought first, then separates what belongs
> in Today from what belongs in Memory. Follow for the iPhone launch.

**B**

> My productivity problem was not planning. It was capturing a thought before
> the “where should I put this?” decision erased it. This is the one-tap voice
> capture flow I built for iPhone.

**Hashtags:** `#braindump #productivitytips #iphoneapps #memorytips`

**Comment prompt:** “When does your brain suddenly remember everything?”

## Concept 2 — The tiny personal detail that actually matters

**Job:** Sell Memory, not task management. The emotional payoff is being a more
thoughtful person, not becoming more productive.

**Creator/setting:** Coffee shop, office kitchen, or dinner table. Use a real
friend/coworker only with permission. A hand, cup, or empty chair is enough;
there is no need to stage someone's face.

| Slide | Visual | Variation A — social story | Variation B — relationship story |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Two coffee cups on a table | **I forgot the one detail that would’ve made me look thoughtful** | **the smallest things are always what I forget about people** |
| 2 | Wrong milk carton or incorrect coffee order | Daniel said “oat milk, not soy.” I knew I’d forget. | birthdays go in calendars. the little preferences have nowhere to go. |
| 3 | Quick phone photo under the table/at counter | so I quietly said: “Daniel takes oat milk, not soy” | so when someone mentions one, I say it into Speak It immediately |
| 4 | Speak It capture/transcript crop with that exact phrase | I did not stop the conversation to make a perfect note | it keeps my original words, even after it tidies the result |

| 6 | Correct oat-milk coffee or a thoughtful real-world payoff | next coffee run, I remembered without pretending I naturally have a perfect memory | I can search the person later instead of scrolling through random notes |
| 7 | Coffee beside phone with Memory open | **Speak It** helps me remember people, not just tasks. *Follow for launch.* | remember the detail while it still means something. **Speak It for iPhone.** |

**A**

> A reminder app can tell me to buy coffee. I wanted somewhere to remember how

**B**

**Creator/setting:** Desk, campus library, commute, grocery aisle. Use mundane

| Slide | Visual | Variation A — contrarian | Variation B — before/after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 2 | Fast sequence represented by one photo collage: Notes, Reminders, Calendar, messages to self | every thought started with: note, task, event, person, or idea? | note? reminder? calendar? message myself? |
| 3 | Empty Notes page or abandoned typed line | by the time I chose, half the sentence was gone | after: tap one button and say the entire thing |
| 4 | Speak It capture screen | Speak It lets me capture first and decide nothing | Speak It handles the first sorting pass after I finish speaking |
| 5 | Today crop | if it needs action, I see it in **Today** | tasks, reminders, and follow-ups → **Today** |
| 6 | Memory crop | if it is worth keeping, I find it in **Memory** | ideas, people, and useful context → **Memory** |
| 7 | Clean desk, phone centered | the best system is the one I can use mid-thought. **Speak It.** | one capture door instead of four. **Speak It for iPhone.** |

### Captions

**A**

> I did not need another complicated productivity system. I needed a faster

**B**

**Creator/setting:** Getting ready in the morning, walking out the door, or
carrying groceries. The creator must look occupied. Avoid using ADHD as a hook
unless the real creator chooses to describe their own diagnosed experience;
the product should not make a medical claim.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## How to publish the two variations

Treat A and B as a clean creative test, not as two posts to dump together.

1. Post Variation A.
2. Wait **48–72 hours** before Variation B so the posts do not cannibalize the
   same initial audience moment.
3. Keep the creator, visual sequence, slide count, posting window, audio mood,
   caption length, and CTA type as similar as practical. The main variable is
   the framing: emotional versus practical.
4. Do not decide from raw views alone. Normalize every outcome by post views.
5. Compare:
   - saves per 1,000 views;
   - shares per 1,000 views;
   - comments that show intent (“name?”, “when can I get it?”, “does it…?”);
   - profile visits per 1,000 views;
   - bio-link clicks and attributed App Store visits/installs;
   - average engagement or dwell metrics available in the account's Photo Mode
     analytics.
6. The winning **hook** can be reused with a new story. The winning **story** can
   be reshot with a new creator. Do not keep reposting an identical slideshow
   after it fatigues.

### Recommended release order

1. Concept 4A — strongest visible demo and broadest relatable moment.
2. Concept 2A — differentiates Speak It from ordinary task apps.
3. Concept 3A — clarifies the “capture before organization” position.
4. Concept 1A — emotionally relatable lifestyle story.
5. Concept 5A — trust-focused proof after viewers know what the product does.
6. Release the B variations in the same order, using what the first wave taught
   you about copy size, screenshots, and CTA language.

## Integrity and platform checks before every post

- Turn on TikTok's **Commercial Content Disclosure** setting. Posts promoting
  the founder's own product are still promotional content under TikTok's rules.
- If a creator was paid, gifted access, or received another incentive, use the
  correct paid-partnership disclosure and any additional disclosure required in
  the target market.
- Use only the Commercial Music Library or properly licensed music.
- A founder demo should say or imply “I built/use this,” not “I found this” if
  that would create a false independent endorsement.
- Only use a real customer's words or results with permission and substantiation.
- Verify the live App Store availability, price, free allowance, and offer before
  including them. The current product contract is ten free captures total, then
  Pro for unlimited captures; the live App Store listing is the authority for
  price.
- Never claim all speech recognition is offline. Apple's Speech framework may
  use an internet connection depending on device and language.
